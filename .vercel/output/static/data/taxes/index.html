Taxes · the short version

Why are taxes going up?

There isn't one cause. Peterborough's city-wide tax requirement has risen as operating costs, capital financing and outside-agency requirements increased, while revenues and one-time reserve draws offset part of the pressure.

One-time funding matters

The 2026 levy was lowered by a $3M Legacy Fund income-reserve draw.

This was accumulated investment income, not a draw on the original PDI-sale principal. But it was still one-time money used against an ongoing budget requirement.

Why it matters
The pressure can return

A one-time offset does not permanently reduce the recurring cost base.

The takeaway

Tax growth is a mix of recurring pressures and temporary offsets.

The useful question isn't simply whether the levy went up. It is which pressures are structural, which are choices, which are externally driven, and which reductions only postpone a future pressure.
