Legacy Fund: recurring income vs. one-time tax support

One-time Legacy-income tax relief happened in 2024 — and again, at a larger scale, in 2026.

The regular policy uses Legacy Fund investment income to support the capital program. Separately, Council has also drawn accumulated Legacy Fund income from reserve to lower the tax requirement: $1.31 million in the final 2024 budget and $3 million in 2026.

The 2026 capital financing plan continues the regular $1.5 million use of Legacy Fund investment income for capital. This is separate from the extra levy offset below.

One-time levy offsets
2024 + 2026

Accumulated income reserve → tax relief

This is a separate mechanism from the regular capital contribution. The 2024 budget used $1.31 million from accumulated Legacy income for tax-rate relief. The 2026 budget used $3 million, which the City says reduced the tax requirement by 1.32 percentage points.

History of Legacy-income levy offsets

The mechanism is not unique to 2026.

The 2024 final budget combined an $800,000 Finance Committee draw with a further $510,000 final-Council draw. The following 2025 budget did not carry that $1.31 million forward. In 2026, Council used the same type of accumulated-income reserve at a larger $3 million scale.

Why this classification matters

A reserve draw can lower the tax requirement in a particular year without lowering the recurring cost base. The 2024 experience illustrates the rollover effect: the $1.31 million tax-rate support was not carried into 2025. The 2026 draw is larger, and the City's preliminary 2027 work identifies a $3 million base-budget correction. The dashboard therefore labels these as one-time funding offsets, separate from the regular Legacy investment-income contribution to capital.
