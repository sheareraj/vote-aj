Outcomes & KPIs

What are we getting for it?

Budgets tell us what City Hall puts into a system. These measures show how key outcomes are moving over time: housing progress, transit use, crime severity, infrastructure condition and pressure in the housing system. Where the City only publishes activities rather than a comparable outcome trend, that limitation is shown explicitly.

Read this carefully: a budget trend beside an outcome does not prove one caused the other. Some outcomes are affected by the economy, senior governments, private investment and community organizations; some Social Services metrics cover both the City and County. The scope and source are shown so those limitations stay visible.

Transit use

Transit ridership and budget figures cover different reporting bases in some years; source labels are shown.

Published trend: Improving
Ridership has recovered from the 2023 level

Reported total ridership rose from 3,205,294 in 2023 to more than 3.7 million in 2025 — a minimum increase of about 15.4%. A 2018 historical reference of 4,973,571 is shown, but 2025 is published only as a rounded lower bound, so no exact recovery-to-peak percentage is claimed.

Method: Uses City-reported all-mode ridership. Comparability is stronger for 2023-2025 than for the older historical reference because service design and counting practices can change.

Police & public safety

The longitudinal crime trend uses Statistics Canada’s Peterborough CMA for consistent boundaries. A separate PPS service-area CSI is shown as current local-service context; the two series are not combined.

Published trend: Improving
Regional Crime Severity Index is lower than in 2021

Statistics Canada’s Peterborough CMA CSI moved from 64.8 in 2021 to 57.3 in 2025, an 11.6% decline, with a small increase in 2024 before falling again in 2025. Lower is better.

Method: The longitudinal series uses the Peterborough CMA for consistent boundaries. The separate PPS service-area CSI is retained as a current service-area metric and should not be spliced into the CMA trend.

Current plan, old condition observation

Housing & homelessness

The City is Service Manager for Peterborough City and County. Social Services is much broader than homelessness spending.

Published trend: Worsening
Latest system count rose 16% in 2025

The City reported a minimum of 932 people experienced homelessness in 2025, up from 803 in 2024 (+16%). The May 2026 shelter report also says demand continues to exceed the existing 127-bed emergency shelter/overnight system.

Method: This is the freshest City-reported system-pressure indicator available. It counts people connected to services who were unsheltered, sheltered or couch surfing; it is not a point-in-time count and should not be compared directly with older RGI waitlist totals.
